01 — The wattle principle

Four
behaviours

It blooms out of season. Acacia pycnantha flowers in late winter, ahead of almost everything else. Being early is only useful if you are also legible — the wattle solves this by being unmistakably gold against a grey paddock. Early and loud, or it may as well not have bloomed.

It colonises disturbed ground. Wattle moves into burnt, cleared, degraded country — the places established species will not touch. Our best engagements look the same: a sector mid-disruption, a regulator mid-rewrite, an institution that has just lost the assumption it was built on.

It fixes nitrogen. Acacias put nitrogen into soil that had none, making the ground viable for the species that follow. This is the part of the metaphor we take most seriously, and it is why every engagement transfers method rather than dependency.

It is fire-adapted. The seed needs heat to germinate. A practice built for stable conditions is worthless in the years that actually matter — we design for the shock, not around it.

Acacia pycnantha — Australia's floral emblem since 1988, and a nitrogen-fixing legume long before anybody put it on a coat of arms.

02 — Structure

How the firm
is built

Small, senior, and deliberately awkward to scale. The constraint is the product.

01

Senior-only rooms

The people in the workshop are the people on the invoice. No pyramid, no analyst layer producing slides for a partner who reads them on the way in.

02

Researchers who ship

Every practitioner both writes analysis and builds software. The two disciplines correct each other — a scenario nobody can prototype is usually a scenario nobody has thought through.

03

Capped engagements

A small number of concurrent clients, with a hard cap. When we are full we say so and give you a date, rather than staffing you with whoever is free.

03 — Position

What modern
futurism means

The term is doing a lot of work, and most of it badly. Here is the version we are willing to defend.

Not prediction

Anyone selling you a single confident forecast about a complex adaptive system is selling comfort. We manufacture options and the means to tell which one is arriving.

Not nostalgia for tomorrow

Twentieth-century futurism was a style — chrome, speed, inevitability. Modern futurism is a practice, and it is mostly unglamorous: reading, arguing, and building small things carefully.

Technology is downstream

Capability rarely decides outcomes. Incentives, regulation, supply chains and installed base decide outcomes. We spend more time on those four than on the demo.

Optimism as a method

Not as a mood. If a preferable future exists, someone has to specify it precisely enough to be built and criticised. That specification is the deliverable.

04 — Colophon

Identity
notes

Wordmark

WATL

Four letters, no vowel between the consonants that matter. Set in Archivo at maximum width and weight — the wide grotesk lineage of Australian civic signage, pushed until the counters close.

Palette

Wattle gold #FFC300 is the only fixed colour. It is always the accent, never the ground, except in the closing slab. Violet, sky, coral, blush and leaf rotate as duotone partners.

#FFC300 #4B2FB8 #FF4A2B #1F6F5C #0A0912

Motif

The bloom is drawn as a rosette of hard circles, never as a soft render. Halftone screens sit over every duotone plate — the flower is a printing problem, not a photograph.

Voice

Declarative. Australian spelling. Short sentences where a long one would hedge. We would rather be wrong in public than vague in private.
